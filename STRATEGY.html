2 · REACT   Where the gap actually sits

The MTD-VAT filing market is commoditised. Xero, Sage, QuickBooks and FreeAgent each ship MTD submission at £14–£33/mo. Bridging tools (#GoFile, 123 Sheets, Avalara MTD file) deliver bare-minimum filing for £0–£20. Pricing power has collapsed at this layer.

But VAT complexity sits in a different layer entirely — and there's no AI-native player there at SME pricing:

Regulatory architecture

UK tax advice is not a reserved activity under the Legal Services Act 2007 — anyone can give it. But complex VAT advice carries professional-indemnity exposure under the HMRC AML-supervised practice regime, and the customer's reliance on it can trigger penalty defences (Sch 24 FA 2007 "reasonable care" / "reasonable excuse").

The architectural decision (mirroring immigration.law's OISC partner pattern and dpo.law's named DPO pattern):

  • vat.law (the platform) = technology + deterministic computation + audit trail + trained QA reviewer. Non-regulated.
  • CTA / CIOT / ATT partner adviser network (3 seed firms) = regulated complex advice on the customer's specific facts. Independent. Sign-off required on any output that crosses into advisory territory.
  • trained QA reviewer = the human-in-the-loop layer between AI and customer. Every regulator-facing output is QA-stamped.
  • Routing = geography → capacity → specialism. Commission firewalled from the routing decision (same pattern as immigration.law).
